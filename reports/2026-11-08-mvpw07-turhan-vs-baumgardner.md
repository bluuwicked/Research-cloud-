## Record / Competition Context

### Turhan: a 14-0 record that is thinner than it looks, but carries real power
- **Fights 1–9 (2022–2024)** were mostly in Albania, Kosovo and North Macedonia against low-level opposition. There were five first-round KOs, plus decisions over Edith Soledad Matthysse, Kirsty Hill and Lara Altamirano.
- **The breakout run (2025):**
  - **Shauna Browne**, TKO 1 (0:47) in Belfast, taken on 10 days' notice and billed as a WBC eliminator. One left hook ended it.
  - **Rima Ayadi** (11-1), TKO 6. A steady body attack, then a right hand.
  - **Beatriz Ferreira**, TKO 5, to win the IBF title. She went in as a big underdog against an unbeaten Olympic silver medalist. She dropped Ferreira with the right hand in R1 (another drop just before was ruled a slip) and finished her in R5. Ferreira adjusted in R3–4 with lateral movement and short angled counters, and Turhan's output dropped while she did. This was the best win of her career, and BoxingScene and The Sporting News named her 2025 Women's Fighter of the Year.
- **The two defenses (2026) were where the doubts started:**
  - **Taylah Gentzen**, split decision (98-92, 96-94 Turhan; 97-93 Gentzen), 10 x 2 min.
    - CompuBox (snippet only, unverified): Turhan landed **58 of 359 (16%)**; Gentzen 40 of 128 (31%).
    - Gentzen boxed off the back foot with a jab, counters and clinching. Turhan got frustrated, swung wide, smothered her work and was warned for rough tactics.
    - Her best rounds were 8–9. Headlines included "Turhan fails to impress" (BoxingScene) and "lackluster first defense" (The Ring).
  - **Gabriela Tellez** (19 years old, 7-0), UD 96-94 x3, 10 x 2 min.
    - Unofficial counts (unverified) had Tellez out-landing her overall **100–75**, while Turhan landed more power shots (55 vs. 40).
    - Tellez won rounds by boxing between Turhan's swings. Turhan hurt her with clubbing rights in R6 and R9 and took the late rounds on strength.
    - Writers called it a fight "that could have gone either way."
- **Takeaway:** Her one elite-level win came by finding the right hand early. Against two mid-level movers who jabbed and countered, she missed a great deal and won close fights on aggression and late physicality.
- **Unknowns:**
  - **Every one of her 14 fights was 2-minute rounds.** Tonight is her first 3-minute fight.
  - No amateur medals could be confirmed in English or Turkish sources.

### Baumgardner: proven at the top level at 130, untested at 135
- **Quality of wins:**
  - **Terri Harper**, KO 4. She went in as a roughly 3-1 underdog; Harper was off a 12-month layoff with a broken right hand.
  - **Mikaela Mayer**, SD. Cards 96-95, 96-95, 93-97.
  - **Elhem Mekhaled**, UD with two knockdowns, to become undisputed.
  - **Christina Linardatou**, UD 99-91, 99-91, 98-92 in the 2023 rematch, avenging her only loss (an 8-round split decision in 2018).
  - Plus decisions over Jennifer Miranda, Leila Beaudoin and Bo Mi Re Shin.
- **Recent form (since the 2023–24 doping case):**
  - Background: a 2023 positive test for mesterolone. The WBC found the use non-intentional, and the suspension was lifted in March 2024.
  - **Persoon (Sep 2024), NC at 4 rounds.** She was controlling with the jab when a head clash cut Persoon.
  - **Miranda (Jul 2025), UD 98-92, 98-92, 97-93.**
    - Flat and low-output after ring rust: about 28 punches per 2-minute round, out-landed 79–78 (snippet only).
    - Miranda's **size and inside power** bothered her. Observers noted she **drops her left when she throws the right**.
    - She graded herself "B+."
  - **Beaudoin (Dec 2025), UD 117-110, 117-110, 118-109 over 12 x 3 min.**
    - She scored a knockdown with a **counter right** in R7.
    - She was briefly shaken in R5 and clinched to recover.
    - She did not fade.
  - **Shin (Apr 2026), UD 98-92, 98-92, 99-91 over 10 x 3 min.**
    - She hurt Shin in R3, and The Ring called it her most complete performance.
    - Secondary-source CompuBox: 279 of 718 landed (39%).
    - Several reports say Shin's right hand **shook her**, and the action was closer than the cards.
- **Takeaways:**
  - Her last six wins all went the distance, and she has **no KO since November 2021**.
  - She is now **3-for-3 in 3-minute-round fights** (counting the one-sided 4 rounds against Persoon at 2 minutes, she has never looked uncomfortable with distance).
- **Common opponent:** Both fighters beat **Edith Soledad Matthysse** by decision. That tells us little.

## Style Matchup

**Turhan** is a forward-moving power puncher.
- **Weapons:** the overhand or clubbing right and the left hook, with heavy body work.
- **Habits:** a loose, wide guard, a jab she rarely uses, single-shot bursts instead of combinations, and rough clinching when frustrated.
- **Rhythm:** a French ringside report described her as "two calm minutes, thirty seconds of storm."

**Baumgardner** is an athletic boxer-puncher and counter-puncher.
- **Offense:** jab, a sharp **counter right**, inside uppercuts and body work.
- **Style:** selective, low-to-moderate volume, high power-punch accuracy, comfortable on the back foot. Her plan for tonight, in her words: "making her miss, making her pay."

How the strengths and weaknesses interact:

1. **Baumgardner is a much better version of the fighters who troubled Turhan.**
   - Gentzen and Tellez frustrated Turhan by moving, jabbing and countering. Neither is near Baumgardner's level of hand speed, timing or punch quality.
   - Turhan's wide right hand is the shot a counter right beats: Baumgardner's counter comes straight down the middle while Turhan's swing loops around the outside.
   - This is the key interaction in the fight, and it favors Baumgardner.
2. **Turhan's offense goes straight at Baumgardner's known defensive flaw.**
   - Baumgardner drops her left when she throws the right. A puncher who throws the overhand right as a counter or a trade can catch her right there.
   - Shin, Beaudoin and Miranda all landed right hands that shook or bothered her, and none of them hit as hard as Turhan.
   - **This is the most realistic upset path.**
3. **Cutting off the ring.**
   - Turhan's main weakness against movers is that she follows instead of cutting angles. Baumgardner does not need to run; she needs to turn and counter.
   - Pressure without footwork tends to get countered by a skilled back-foot boxer.
4. **Inside and clinch.**
   - Turhan is likely the stronger fighter inside, and she uses rough tactics.
   - Miranda showed that size and inside power at close range can neutralize Baumgardner's timing.
   - If Turhan makes it a phone-booth fight with clinches, rounds become close and ugly. That shape favors Turhan more than a fight at range does.
   - How the referee handles holding and rough work matters.
5. **Round length helps Baumgardner.**
   - The extra minute per round gives the counter-puncher more time to set traps and bank clean exchanges in each round.
   - Turhan's burst style was built for 2-minute rounds, and she has never had to carry that pressure for 3. She has finished strong in 2-minute fights, so this is a question, not a proven weakness.
   - Baumgardner has now gone 22 rounds of 3 minutes in her last two fights without fading.
6. **Moving up in weight cuts both ways.**
   - Baumgardner's power at 135 is the open question; Max Boxing called it the key variable.
   - Her power is already more about timing than raw force (no KO since 2021), so she probably will not drop Turhan with single shots the way she did at 130.
   - Her defense at 135 is untested too: she has never been hit by a natural 135-pounder with Turhan's power.

## Physical & Current Factors

- **Size:**
  - Turhan is the naturally bigger fighter, with about +1 inch of height and +3 inches of reach on the listed numbers. She has fought from 130 to 140.
  - Both weighed in under the limit (Baumgardner 133.8 lb). Neither appears to have struggled with weight.
- **Age:** Close (31 vs. 32). Not a factor.
- **Activity:** Both are reasonably active. Turhan fought three times in about 10 months. Baumgardner's gap of about 7 months is fine, and she has looked sharper since the post-suspension rust of the Miranda fight.
- **Home advantage:** The fight is in Baumgardner's Dallas–Fort Worth backyard ("this is my backyard"). Expect crowd and possibly judging lean in close rounds.
- **Turhan in the US:**
  - She has fought in the US once, a narrow win over Tellez in Arizona.
  - She has had **only about 5 months** since a hard 10-rounder.
  - Her defense against Gentzen came just 8 weeks after the Ferreira fight and was flat.
- **Injuries / camp news:** Nothing reported for either fighter.
- **Baumgardner's trainer for this camp:** Not confirmed. Derrick James was in her corner in 2025.

## Path to Victory: Turhan

- **Primary path: knockdowns or a stoppage from the right hand.**
  - Throw the overhand right over or after Baumgardner's right hand, aiming at the dropped left.
  - Mix in the left hook to the body to slow Baumgardner's movement.
  - One or two knockdowns would also swing a decision (10-8 rounds).
- **Secondary path: make it ugly and win late.**
  - Walk her down, lean on her, smother her at close range and win rounds 7–10 on strength, as she did against Gentzen and Tellez.
  - Takes close rounds with judges who reward aggression.
- **Conditions needed:**
  - Use more of a jab or feints to close distance instead of loading up from range.
  - Cut off the ring to the left and right instead of following.
  - Keep her pressure going through 3-minute rounds.
- **Failure point:**
  - Missing wide from range, so every lunge earns a counter right. That is what happened against Gentzen and Tellez, and Baumgardner punishes it harder.
  - If she is down 4–5 rounds by the halfway mark, she needs the KO.

## Path to Victory: Baumgardner

- **Primary path: a counter-punching decision.**
  - Use the jab, then step left away from the right hand.
  - Time the counter right and uppercut as Turhan loads up, then turn out before the clinch.
  - Take each 3-minute round by landing the cleaner punches with fewer misses.
- **Secondary path: a late stoppage.**
  - Accumulated counters plus body work on a fighter who has never gone 30 minutes in a fight. Rounds 7–10 are the danger window for Turhan if her pace drops.
- **Conditions needed:**
  - Defensive discipline on the right hand. Bring the left back home and don't stay in the pocket after landing.
  - Don't get pinned on the ropes, where Turhan's hooks are most dangerous (that is how Ferreira was finished).
- **Failure point:** Getting dragged into exchanges or clinch fights where Turhan's size and power cancel out her timing (the Miranda shape of fight), or eating a clean right she does not see coming.

## Prediction

**Most likely winner: Alycia Baumgardner, by decision (unanimous most likely).**

| Outcome | Estimated probability |
|---|---|
| **Baumgardner wins** | **~72%** |
| — by decision | ~57% |
| — by KO/TKO (likely late) | ~15% |
| **Turhan wins** | **~26%** |
| — by KO/TKO | ~17% |
| — by decision | ~9% |
| Draw | ~2% |

**Most likely shape of the fight:**
- Turhan has her moments early with wild rights.
- Baumgardner settles by rounds 3–4 and counters the right hand cleanly through the middle rounds.
- Turhan rallies late on strength.
- Something like **97-93 / 96-94 Baumgardner**, possibly with a knockdown at some point. A knockdown for either fighter is a realistic prop: Turhan drops people, and Baumgardner's counter right dropped Beaudoin at 3-minute-round pace.

**How this compares with the market:**
- Sportsbooks have Baumgardner at about **-450 to -700**, roughly an 82–87% implied chance.
- My estimate is lower because:
  - Turhan has the natural size and genuine one-punch power.
  - Baumgardner has been shaken by right hands in each of her last three full fights, against opponents who hit less hard.
  - It is Baumgardner's first fight at 135.
- So Turhan's upset chance is meaningful, not remote. Most outlets also pick Baumgardner by decision: Yahoo / Big Fight Weekend, Covers, Max Boxing (a slight lean) and SBR. ESPN's panel was reportedly split.

## Confidence / Main Uncertainty

**Confidence: moderate.** The style matchup clearly favors Baumgardner: an elite counter-puncher against a crude, wide-swinging pressure fighter who struggles to cut off the ring. The method (decision) is the more confident part of the call.

**What creates uncertainty:**
1. **Baumgardner's chin and power at 135.** Neither has been tested at this weight.
2. **Turhan in 3-minute rounds.** No data; this could help her by giving her more time to land, or hurt her through fatigue.
3. **Inside and clinch dynamics, and the referee.** If the fight becomes a mauling, it gets much closer.
4. **Data quality.**
   - Several CompuBox figures for both fighters came from search snippets or secondary sites, not first-hand CompuBox pages.
   - Tapology, BoxRec, box.live, BoxingScene and Bad Left Hook were blocked to automated access, so the records were checked against Wikipedia, MVP and news reports instead.
